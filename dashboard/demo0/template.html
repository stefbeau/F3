F3 Demo 0: AI, electricity and climate, 2025-2100

Conditional scenarios, not forecasts. Every curve below is the output of a model run under stated assumptions. None is a prediction, none has a likelihood, and this page is a local draft that has not been approved for publication (plan test A2.7).
Read before using any number
  • AI sector (S3). It does not reproduce GATE. GATE was re-implemented from the paper, not from its code. Epoch states that GATE behaves poorly near full automation.
  • Climate (FaIR). FaIR climate starts about 0.04 to 0.07 K below observed for the current decade (D-030); non-CO2 emissions are prescribed, not modelled (D-021). The test of FaIR against HadCRUT5 for 2015-2024 failed (ensemble mean 0.037 K below the interval).
  • Damage options. All three are level-of-output effects. The "high" option is NOT the upper end of the literature: growth-effect estimates are larger and are not represented (D-007).
  • Investment rule R3 is a scenario-lever rule, not a GATE replica (D-028). Against the three shipped GATE runs, with default levers and Epoch's stored composite supplied, effective compute is 43% to 72% off at worst, output 13% to 20% off, hardware up to 35% off, and the share errors reach 24 percentage points (capital) and 55 (training share) (A1.4, report only, audit/s3-r3-a1.4.md).
  • Data-centre electricity. 485 TWh (2025) is a calibration point and 415 TWh (2024) a reference value; neither validates the module, and no independent test of modelled data-centre electricity against estimates exists before 2025. The IEA 2030 figure (950 TWh) is the IEA's projection under its own assumptions, shown for comparison only. Boundary: a data centre is a facility housing servers, storage, networking equipment and associated components (IEA, Energy and AI); whether crypto-mining load is excluded from the IEA totals is [unverified].

Choose a scenario

1. Compute and automation (S3, unvalidated after about 2027)

Raw compute stock (FLOP/year, log scale) and automated fraction of tasks, for the three AI settings at the chosen damage option. Absolute compute is unvalidated against GATE after about 2027; shapes are more reliable than levels.

2. Data-centre electricity

TWh per year, log scale, chosen AI setting, central damage. "Demand" is what the installed compute would draw; "used" is capped by the share of electricity supply available to data centres (5% of the recorded Earth4All.jl TLTL electricity production, a provisional assumption). Dots: IEA 2024 (415, reference) and 2025 (485, calibration point). Diamond: IEA 2030 projection (950), the IEA's projection, not a target. Values after about 2027 inherit S3's unvalidated output; the demand curve leaves any plausible range quickly, which is a property of S3's compute path, not a finding about the world.

3. Temperature under the three damage options

FaIR warming above 1850-1900, ensemble of 841 members: mean (line) and 5th-95th percentile (band). Prescribed emissions do not respond to any lever here, so the temperature path is the same for every setting and damage option: the damage options change output, not temperature, in this slice. The band is the FaIR ensemble band only (not an uncertainty analysis of the whole model). FaIR starts about 0.04 to 0.07 K below observed for the current decade.
Damage fraction = c x T^2 (c per option below). Output losses are shown as shares of S3's own output, whose level is unvalidated.

What is not shown

  • Population, inequality, social stability, minerals, water and the full economy (these come in later milestones). The slice's economy is GATE's own, with population exogenous.
  • Uncertainty bands beyond the FaIR ensemble band; any calibration beyond the stated checks (the 2025 data-centre calibration point and the warming check, which failed); any claim about likelihood.
  • The growth-effect damage case. Climate emissions are prescribed; AI electricity does not feed back into emissions in this slice.
  • Open caveats: GATE is re-implemented from its paper and not from its code; Epoch states GATE behaves poorly near full automation; FaIR is used as published; nothing has been validated against an independent reference beyond the tests A1 and A2 and their recorded failures.

Recorded test results (development ensemble, 20 members)

  • A2.4 invariance, A2.5 extreme conditions, A2.2 consistency check, R1 and B2 signs: see tests/test_m2_slice.py outcomes in docs/tracks/track-a.md.
  • A2.6 step test (0.25 vs 0.125 year, limit 0.1%): failed (largest difference 0.56% in data-centre electricity, first above 0.1% in 2028).
  • B1 sign: failed as written (a lower supply case had up to 17.8% more compute in 2038-2045).
  • R1 sign: failed on automation at 2065 (both runs at 1.0); compute is higher.
Details and exact sizes: docs/tracks/track-a.md and audit/data/m2-slice-results-dev20.json. This page uses all 841 FaIR members for the climate.
