# L3 — Climate to yield and health (draft, D-058)

**Status of this page:** data and literature plan, written before any code or test run. No result is reported here. Provisional (delegated) under D-058. Numbers from papers the campaign could not read are marked **[from memory, unverified]**; do not use them without reading the paper.

If the editor numbers them differently, only the file names change.

## What must connect
Warming from `s6_climate` (K above pre-industrial, FaIR) to (a) crop yield, entering World3 agriculture (land yield factor) and food per capita, and (b) heat-related mortality, entering population (S1) through the death rate. Today the only built climate coupling is damage to industrial output (default off); yield and health are unbuilt.

## Candidate variables and units
| Variable | Unit | Source series |
|---|---|---|
| Global mean warming `T` | K above 1850-1900 | `hadcrut5_global_annual` (observed), FaIR (model) |
| Cereal yield | kg/ha, world | `faostat_world_cereals` (1961-2024); cross-checked with WDI (0.02% apart in 2023) |
| Population | persons, world | `wpp2024_world_population` |
| Yield change per K | % per K by crop | Jagermeyr 2021 (abstract only read), Zhao 2017, Schlenker and Roberts 2009 (not read) |
| Heat-related deaths | deaths per year | Lancet Countdown (NC-SA: raw only, not usable), Vicedo-Cabrera 2021, Gasparrini 2017 (not read) |
| Economic damage | % of output per K | Kalkuhl and Wenz 2020 (retrieved), Burke 2015 (not read), Carleton 2022 (not read) |

## Functional forms in the literature
1. **Linear or quadratic yield response to temperature**, as used in the crop meta-analyses: yield change of the order of -3 to -7 % per K of global warming for wheat, rice, maize, soy [from memory, unverified; Zhao et al. 2017, PNAS]. Test against `faostat_world_cereals` only after removing the technology trend.
2. **Piecewise (degree-day) response with a damage threshold** around 29-30 C for maize and soy [from memory, unverified; Schlenker and Roberts 2009]. Needs gridded temperature, which F3 does not have.
3. **Process-model ensembles under CO2 and adaptation** (Jagermeyr et al. 2021, Nature Food): the abstract numbers were read, the supplement was not. They give a range, not a function.
4. **Mortality: exposure-response curves with a minimum-mortality temperature, relative risk rising with heat** [from memory, unverified; Gasparrini et al. 2017, Lancet Planet Health; Vicedo-Cabrera et al. 2021, Nat Clim Change]. Needs temperature distributions, not a global mean.
5. **Output damage as a function of warming** (alternative route, already the shape D-007 and the built damage coupling use): Kalkuhl and Wenz 2020 give panel estimates with level and growth effects; Burke 2015's quadratic in temperature is the usual comparison [from memory, unverified].

## Calibration data available (retrieved)
- Observed warming and a long world cereal yield series with population and WDI food indicators (1961-2024).
- FAOSTAT area, yield and production for cereals (derived: world level only; the raw bulk is not committed).
- IPCC AR6 WG1 Interactive Atlas datasets (partial, CC BY 4.0) for regional warming patterns.
- ISIMIP3b: a small subset only (CC0); crop and health outputs were **not** downloaded.

## Pass/fail test (written without results)
Pre-registered, to be fixed before any code:
1. **Sign and size.** With the coupling on and a warming path from the FaIR ensemble, world cereal yield in 2100 relative to the coupling-off run must be lower, and the ensemble median of the yield change per K must lie inside the range reported by the papers once they are read (range to be fixed from the papers, not from the run). Fail if it lies outside or has the wrong sign.
2. **Switch-off invariance.** With the coupling off, the sector returns its stand-alone result to 1e-9 (the A6.3 pattern).
3. **Hindcast sanity.** Over 1961-2024 the coupling with observed warming must not change modelled cereal yield by more than the plausible warming effect (a few per cent), judged against the FAOSTAT trend; this is a bound test, not a fit. Fail if it exceeds the bound fixed in advance.
4. **No double counting.** If the output-damage coupling is on at the same time, record the combined effect separately and state both channels.
