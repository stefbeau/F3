# L4 — AI and automation to labour and income (draft, D-058)

**Status of this page:** data and literature plan, written before any code or test run. No result is reported. Provisional (delegated). See L3.md for the link numbering used. Numbers from unread papers are marked **[from memory, unverified]**.

## What must connect
The automation fraction from S3 (AI sector) to the labour share of income and the labour index in S2/S7 (inequality, social tension). The labour-index link from S3 exists in a diagnostic form (D-055, `s3-supply` branch, not merged); the empirical anchor for its size is missing.

## Candidate variables and units
| Variable | Unit | Source series |
|---|---|---|
| Labour share of income | fraction of GDP | `pwt_labour_share` (PWT 11.0 `labsh`, 1950-2023; median 2023 0.5195), `ilostat_labour_income_share` (ILO, from 2004; median about 46%) |
| Employment by sector | persons or % | `ilostat_world_employment_by_sector` |
| Task exposure to AI | score 0-1 | `task_exposure_summary` (Anthropic Economic Index observed exposure, mean 0.077; GPTs-are-GPTs ratings) |
| GDP per capita | 2011 USD | `maddison_world_gdp_per_capita`, `wdi_world_series` |
| AI revenue | USD per year | `epoch_ai_company_revenue` |
| Conflict count | per year | `ucdp_conflicts_per_year` (for the S7 link) |

The two labour-share series differ by 11% at the 2019 median (`labour_share_pwt_vs_ilo_2019`): definitions and samples differ, so one of them must be chosen by decision, not averaged.

## Functional forms in the literature
1. **Task-based model** (Acemoglu and Restrepo): automation displaces labour from tasks and lowers the labour share, new tasks restore it; the labour share is a function of the share of tasks automated [from memory, unverified against the retrieved NBER paper; the paper is in the raw folder].
2. **Capital-labour substitution with elasticity above one** as the explanation of the post-1980 decline in the labour share (Karabarbounis and Neiman 2014; the retrieved 2013 NBER version) [from memory, unverified].
3. **Bounded macro effect of AI** (Acemoglu 2024): total-factor-productivity gains computed as exposure times cost saving times share of tasks, giving small figures over ten years [from memory, unverified].
4. **Occupation exposure scores** (Eloundou et al. 2023; Felten AIOE, licence unclear): exposure is an input to a labour-share function, not a result.
5. **Observed usage** (Anthropic Economic Index; Brynjolfsson et al. 2025): early-career employment in exposed occupations, a direct but US-only signal.

## Calibration data available (retrieved)
PWT and ILO labour-share histories; ILO sectoral employment; Anthropic and GPTs-are-GPTs exposure (Spearman 0.70 observed versus GPT-4 beta, 0.67 versus human beta, as read from the data); O*NET 31.0 task database; Epoch company revenues; UCDP conflicts; WID bulk file (licence unclear, raw only).

## What is missing
- **A measured automation effect on the labour share.** The labour-share histories predate generative AI; the exposure data start in 2023. There is no time series linking the two.
- OECD AI policy data (terms page blocked), WID legal terms, the ILOSTAT terms detail (an ILO CC BY 4.0 statement was found; see register).
- Country-level mapping from exposure to employment weights.
- The papers Burke 2015 and Dunne 2013 are listed in needs-user for the heat-productivity channel.

## Pass/fail test (written without results)
1. **Direction and bound.** With automation fraction raised, the labour share falls, and the fall over 2025-2060 stays inside a bound fixed in advance from the historical range of PWT (for example, no larger than the largest 30-year fall in the world median in 1950-2023; the number is read from `pwt_labour_share` before the code is written, then frozen).
2. **Level.** The 2025 modelled labour share lies within the spread between the PWT and ILO medians. Fail otherwise.
3. **Switch-off invariance** to 1e-9 (A6.3 pattern).
4. **Sensitivity honesty.** The report states that the automation-to-share elasticity has no empirical anchor in the retrieved data; a run that presents it as calibrated fails.

## Badge it would earn
**Amber, "Unvalidated: levels anchored, response not calibrated"**: the starting level can be matched to PWT/ILO; the response to AI cannot be tested with the retrieved data. Until a decision picks a labour-share definition, **red, "Not built"**.
