# L5 — Compute to electricity (draft, D-058)

**Status of this page:** data and literature plan, written before any code or test run. No result is reported. Provisional (delegated). Link numbering as in L3.md. See DC.md for the data-centre module itself; L5 is the supply chain from chips to MW.

## What must connect
AI compute (S3, FLOP per year) to electricity demand of data centres (S4 module, TWh per year), through hardware efficiency (FLOP per joule), chip stock, utilisation, and facility overhead (PUE). The existing module is D-032/D-033 (Track A); this page is the empirical side.

## Functional forms in the literature
1. **Bottom-up stock model:** energy = sum over chips of (TDP x utilisation x hours) x PUE (Epoch GATE paper and LBNL 2024 use variants) [from memory, unverified against the retrieved files; the Epoch GATE PDF and LBNL report are in the raw folder].
2. **Hardware efficiency as an exponential trend** (FLOP/J doubling in roughly 2 to 3 years) [from memory; test on `epoch_hardware_efficiency`, which has the actual points].
3. **Scenario envelopes** (IEA Base, Lift-Off, High Efficiency, Headwinds): Lift-Off over 1,260 TWh and Headwinds about 670 TWh in 2030 (read from the report, with page numbers in `iea_energy_ai_figures`). These are scenarios, not forecasts, and not data.
4. **Energy per training run versus per inference** (Luccioni 2022 for a single model lifecycle) [from memory, unverified].

## Calibration data available (retrieved)
Epoch chips (quarterly stock and sales), hardware efficiency, data-centre timelines 2018-2030, GPU clusters; IEA 2024 level and four scenarios; LBNL US 176 TWh in 2023 (4.4% of US electricity), range 325 to 580 TWh in 2028 (PDF, licence unclear, cited not committed); Ember electricity; hyperscaler reports (unclear licence, cited by page).

## What is missing
- A **second independent measure** of data-centre electricity. The IEA 1.5% share sits on its own denominator (cross-check `dc_share_iea_vs_ember`: 1.34% on Ember's, a 10% gap). The IEA data product is behind a login (needs-user, priority 1).
- Utilisation and measured power draw per chip (not checked whether the Epoch files carry only rated power).
- Regional split below the IEA's three regions (US 45%, China 25%, Europe 15%).
- Epoch's 95 tracked frontier sites draw about 70% less power than the IEA average load for all data centres (`dc_scale_epoch_vs_iea`): different scope; not a disagreement but also not a bridge.

## Pass/fail test (written without results)
1. **Level.** Modelled data-centre electricity in 2024 lies within 415 TWh +/- a tolerance fixed before the run (propose the spread between IEA and LBNL-implied global scaling; the editor sets it).
2. **Trajectory envelope.** 2030 lies between the IEA Headwinds and Lift-Off values (about 670 and over 1,260 TWh) in the central run; ensemble outside this envelope is reported, not repaired.
3. **Efficiency bound.** Modelled FLOP/J never exceeds the best Epoch point by more than the pre-set factor in the historical window.
4. **Switch-off invariance** (A6.3 pattern).
5. The run is labelled **S3 unvalidated against GATE after about 2027** (D-029) regardless of these results.

## Badge it would earn
