# M2 step 1: FaIR alone (A2.1) and the climate sector `s6_climate`

**Status:** plan written 2026-10-12, **before any code and before any comparison**. Tests and the exact reading of A2.1 below are fixed now. This step does not use S3 (D-029 labelling is not triggered); the sector's own docstring states its validation status. Not in this step: the data-centre module, the damage function (D-007 is open), the coupled slice.

## 1. What the plan and decisions require

- **A2.1** (`docs/phase-2-plan.md`, M2): "With recorded historical emissions (Global Carbon Budget 2025 for CO2; the source chosen in N2 for the rest), FaIR's mean warming for 2015-2024 lies within the observational uncertainty of the observed series chosen (HadCRUT or NOAA...; the uncertainty is read from the dataset). FaIR's configuration is taken as published, with its calibration source recorded."
- **D-021:** FaIR is re-run from the start each year; state carried between segments is not used. Non-CO2 emissions and land-use CO2 are prescribed; every result that depends on them is labelled "non-CO2 emissions are prescribed, not modelled".
- **D-020 / D-022:** FaIR runs from its own history with prescribed historical emissions; loop step 0.25 year; `t = Y` is 1 January of year Y, stocks are values on that date (FaIR temperature sits on time bounds, which are 1 January); coupled results are reported from 1980.
- **D-014 / D-026 pattern** for third-party reference data (read at test time from a pinned local copy, sha256 recorded, skip with a stated reason when absent, fail visibly on a hash change).

## 3. Configuration and inputs (choices where the plan is silent)

1. **Ensemble.** All 841 members of the published constrained ensemble, unweighted (the posterior is already resampled to the target distributions), with each member's own stochastic internal variability **on and seeded**, exactly as the published parameter file specifies. Volcanic and solar forcing are scaled per member as in the FaIR example notebook (`forcing_scale[Volcanic]`, `forcing_scale[Solar]`). FaIR options as in the notebook: `FAIR(ch4_method="Thornhill2021")`, annual step, 1750 start, initial conditions as the notebook sets them. **No parameter is changed.**
2. **Emissions.** All species and all years up to 2022 are taken from the file the ensemble was constrained with (the "extensions" file, whose scenarios are identical until 2023). **CO2 for 2023 and 2024** (FFI and AFOLU) is taken from GCB 2025 (fossil emissions excluding carbonation as CO2 FFI; land-use change as CO2 AFOLU; GtC x 44.009/12.011 = 3.6641 to Gt CO2), as A2.1 states. **Non-CO2 species for 2023 and 2024, and all species from 2025, come from the scenario `medium-extension`** of that file (prescribed, not modelled; D-021). Why not replace the whole CO2 history with GCB 2025: the ensemble was constrained against the CO2 series in the file; replacing it would change what was calibrated. A **report-only sensitivity** replaces 2015 to 2024 with GCB 2025 throughout.
3. **Baseline.** Anomalies are against the 1850-1900 mean, computed as the FaIR example notebook does (time-bound values with weights 0.5, 1, ..., 1, 0.5 over bounds 1850 to 1901), per member. HadCRUT5 is re-based to the same period by subtracting the mean of its annual best estimates for 1850 to 1900.
4. **Annual values.** FaIR temperatures are on time bounds (1 January). The calendar-year value used to compare with observations is the average of the bounds at the start and end of the year, as the notebook does. The sector publishes the 1 January value (D-022).
5. **Cost.** D-021 asks for the cost of the re-run rule with a real configuration, which was not measured. The report measures it (time per annual re-run, 20 and 841 members) and projects a full 1980-2100 loop.

## 4. Acceptance tests (fixed before any comparison)

**A2.1 as read here.** Let `F` = the mean over 2015 to 2024 of the **ensemble-mean** FaIR calendar-year anomaly (all 841 members, baseline 1850-1900). Let `[lo, hi]` = the mean over 2015 to 2024 of HadCRUT5's lower and upper 95% limits, each re-based to 1850-1900 as in 3.3. **Pass if `lo <= F <= hi`.** The interval is read from the dataset and is the mean of ten annual intervals (an upper bound on the width of a decadal-mean uncertainty if annual errors are independent, exact if fully correlated); it ignores the baseline's own uncertainty. A failure is reported with its size and stops this step; a changed reading is a labelled post-hoc proposal, not an adjustment.

**Report-only beside A2.1 (no pass or fail):** each year 2015-2024 (ensemble mean, 5th and 95th percentile of members, HadCRUT5 interval); 2015-2022 (inside the calibration's constraint window) and 2023-2024 (outside it) separately; sensitivity to the non-CO2 scenario (all seven scenarios of the file) and to the CO2 source (3.2); the in-sample caveat: **the ensemble was constrained to observations up to 2022 including HadCRUT5, so A2.1 over 2015-2022 is not an independent test of FaIR's physics; it tests that this run (configuration, emissions, baseline handling) reproduces the published calibration. Only 2023 and 2024 are out of sample.**

**Sector tests (`tests/test_s6_climate.py`), also fixed now:**
- S1. **D-021 equivalence:** the sector's published temperature at every year boundary equals one single FaIR run's time-bound value (same members, same inputs), maximum difference **<= 1e-9 K** (the probe in D-021 found 0.0 for the re-run rule).
- S2. **Time convention (D-022):** the value published at `t = Y` (1 January) is the time-bound anomaly at Y; within a year the published value is held and changes only at year boundaries; a run starting in 1980 reports from 1980.
- S3. **Properties:** every output finite; 5th percentile <= mean <= 95th percentile at every year; two identical runs give identical output.
- S4. **Sign:** zero CO2 emissions from 2026 gives lower warming in 2050 than the scenario run, with the other species unchanged.
- S5. **Data integrity:** the committed GCB extract matches its recorded source hash and the recorded values; the FaIR and HadCRUT5 files, when present, pass their hash checks (a changed hash fails visibly); the tests that need them are skipped with a stated reason when absent.

## 5. Data handling

- `audit/t0/fair_data_fetch.py` downloads the four FaIR files from the pinned commit URL and the HadCRUT5 annual file into `.fair_cache/` (git-ignored; the location can be set with `F3_FAIR_DATA`), checks each hash, and fails with a clear message on a mismatch. HadCRUT5's hash is over the header and the rows for years up to 2024, **as downloaded text**; the whole-file hash is printed for information only.
- `audit/t0/gcb_extract.py` (needs `openpyxl`, development group only) reads the ICOS xlsx and writes `f3/data/gcb2025_co2_2015_2024.csv` with its attribution, the source hash and the conversion factor.
- CI: a separate job fetches the data and runs the FaIR tests; a failed download or a changed hash fails the job. The existing job stays green without the data (the tests skip).
- Stored in the repository: comparison figures and the GCB extract. Never the FaIR calibration files or the HadCRUT5 series.

## 6. Sector design (`f3/sectors/s6_climate.py`)

A `Sector` with `period = 1.0` (advanced every fourth 0.25-year step), no inputs in this step (all emissions prescribed; the coupling of CO2 from the other sectors comes with the slice), outputs published at 1 January: `warming` (K above 1850-1900, ensemble mean), `warming_p05`, `warming_p95`, `co2_concentration` (ppm, ensemble mean). `advance(Y, 1.0)` runs FaIR from 1750 to Y+1 with the prescribed emissions through year Y and publishes the time-bound values at Y+1 (D-021: nothing carried). The ensemble size is a parameter (default all 841; tests use 20 for speed except where A2.1 needs the full ensemble). Validation status in its docstring and as a class attribute, written after the results. The baseline of `warming` against Earth4All's observed-warming constant (D-023: `OW2022 = 1.35`) is **not** checked here and stays open.
