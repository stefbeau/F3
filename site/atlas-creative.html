Creative work and copyright

AI models were trained on books, images and music made by people. Courts are now deciding whether that was lawful, and who gets paid.

Largest settlement$1.5 bnbetween authors and one AI developer, the largest known US copyright payout1
First rulings on training2US federal judges ruled in 2025, both largely for the AI developers3
Lawsuits pendingDozensover text, images, music, code and news2

What is happening

In the United States. Two federal judges issued the first substantive rulings in 2025, in cases brought by authors against Anthropic and Meta. Both treated training on books as fair use in the cases before them, and both judges found the use highly transformative.1 The rulings concerned training only and left open what happens when outputs resemble or compete with the originals.6 One of those cases still ended in a $1.5 billion settlement over how the books were obtained.1 Decisions in further cases are expected, and the central question is not settled.2

In the United Kingdom. Getty Images largely failed in November 2025 to obtain a ruling against Stability AI over training on its photographs, and was later allowed to appeal part of the ruling.4

Licensing. Some disputes are turning into deals. Warner Music settled with two AI music companies and plans joint products with them.1 Lawyers expect more licensing agreements between rights holders and developers.5

Who owns the output. US law still requires a human author. Work produced entirely by AI is generally not protected, while work made with AI assistance may be.5

The ledger

What is gained

  • New tools for writing, images, music and video, at low cost.
  • A licensing market that pays some rights holders.1

What it costs

  • Works used for training without consent or payment, which the first rulings mostly allowed.3
  • Competition from machine-made content in markets where creators sell.6

What is not known

  • The effect on creators’ incomes. No measured figure was found for this atlas.
  • How appeal courts will rule. None has decided the core question yet.
  • Whether licensing will reach individual creators or mainly large catalogues.

F3 and this topic

Not in the model

F3 does not model this. The AI sector treats all tasks in the economy as one continuum and has no separate creative sector, no prices for creative work and no law.
