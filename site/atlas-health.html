Health and science

Medicine has adopted AI quickly, and in trials doctors decide better with it. Proof that patients end up healthier is still rare.

Research output13.7 millionAI publications indexed by OpenAlex6

Evidence behind 1,357 cleared AI medical devices

What is happening

In the clinic. The tools gaining ground are those that fit existing work, such as AI scribes that write up consultations and systems that warn of sepsis.1 In one trial with 238 providers, scribes cut documentation time by nearly 10%.5 A randomised trial found that physicians using AI alongside their usual resources made better treatment decisions.4

The evidence gap. Most devices reach the market through routes that need no new clinical trial.1 A 2026 census traced every cleared device: 34 of 1,357 were linked to a registered prospective trial, and three had published results on outcomes such as death or illness.32

In the lab. AI now designs proteins and models cells, and leading systems outscore most physicians on structured clinical tests. Nearly half of clinical AI studies still use simulated cases and not real patient data.1

The ledger

What is gained

  • Less paperwork for clinicians.5
  • Better decisions in trials when doctors work with AI.4
  • Faster discovery in biology and drug design.1

What it costs

  • Tools in use whose effect on patients has not been measured.2
  • Trials that exist are often small and leave out vulnerable groups.2
  • Risk of bias when tools are trained on unrepresentative data.

What is not known

  • Whether accuracy on tests becomes better health in practice.
  • Who is accountable when an AI-supported decision is wrong.
  • How many AI-designed drugs will pass clinical trials.

F3 and this topic

Not in the model

F3 does not model this. The site’s AI Impact page shows counts of AI publications, notable models and cleared medical devices. Those are data, not model output.
