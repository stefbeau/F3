Land, metals, chips and waste

The cloud is made of buildings, copper and chips that are replaced every few years. Each leaves a physical trace.

Electronic waste in context

What is happening

Land. The largest campus under construction covers about 3,650 acres of former farmland after a 1,400-acre expansion in 2026.1 That is local land-use change on the scale of a small town.

A claim to treat with care: no sourced figure for forest clearing by data centres was found for this atlas. The land figure above is for farmland.

Metals. Data centres could need about 1.1 million tonnes of copper a year by 2030. World demand for refined copper is about 28 million tonnes, and a shortfall is forecast for 2026.5

Waste. Servers and accelerators are replaced quickly. A study in Nature Computational Science projects 1.2 to 5 million tonnes of e-waste from generative AI between 2023 and 2030, and up to 2.5 million tonnes a year by 2030 in its high scenario.23 That is a small share of all e-waste, but it contains lead, mercury and chromium as well as recoverable gold and copper.4

Chips. Making the chips has its own footprint. One industry analyst puts emissions from chip fabrication at 178 million tonnes of CO₂-equivalent in 2026 and 197 million in 2027, with AI and memory demand growing faster than efficiency improves.6 An average chip factory can use about 10 million US gallons of ultra-pure water a day.7 In Taiwan one manufacturer alone used about 8% of the island’s electricity, according to a 2024 report.8

The ledger

What is gained

  • Reuse and longer hardware lifetimes can cut the waste stream substantially; the study models such measures.2
  • Construction brings jobs and tax revenue to rural areas.

What it costs

  • Farmland and open land converted to industrial use.
  • Added pressure on copper supply already in deficit.5
  • Hazardous waste, much of it processed informally in poorer countries.

What is not known

  • Total land taken worldwide. No global figure was found.
  • Effects on forests and wildlife.
  • How much retired hardware is actually recycled.

What this does and does not say. World3’s stock is one lump with no copper, no chips and no land. Nothing in F3 yet connects the reported demand from data centres to it. Land and water use by data centres are planned as reported outputs of the electricity module (decision D-066, step E4). They are not built.
