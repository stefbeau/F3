Learning, minds and information

AI has entered classrooms, bedrooms and news feeds faster than research can follow. The early evidence points both ways.

Students using AI weekly45.5%of 15-year-olds in OECD countries, to help them learn1
Measured learning gain+0.25 SDon a test taken without AI, in one randomised trial2
US teens using chatbots64%and nearly half of 9 to 12-year-olds have tried one6

Learning

In a randomised experiment with 210 undergraduates, access to AI while learning a new topic raised scores on a later test taken without it.2 Trials in Ghana, Nigeria and Sierra Leone found that AI-supported programmes can raise learning, while a large trial in rural China found no gain from AI personalisation over ordinary practice.3

The research base is thin. Of 1,686 papers in one Stanford repository, 52 tested a specific intervention.4 The open question is whether students learn more or only produce better-looking work.3

Wellbeing

Chatbots are part of daily life for most American teenagers, and use starts years before the minimum age on most services.5 A survey of 39,761 Ontario students found that 57.7% of those who turned to AI for emotional support met the criteria for serious emotional problems, against 29.2% of those who did not.7 That is an association: distressed young people may simply seek support more. A Stanford study suggests AI companions may worsen loneliness for vulnerable users.8

Information

The World Economic Forum ranks misinformation and disinformation among the top short-term global risks. The evidence is mixed on whether deepfakes persuade better than other content, but they make it harder to know what is real and give liars a way to dismiss what is true.9 UK fact-checkers documented 112 pieces of AI-made or AI-altered misinformation seen tens of millions of times in fifteen months.10 A regulator's review found emerging evidence that language models can be politically persuasive.11

The ledger

What is gained

  • Tutoring at low cost, with measured gains in some trials.2
  • Support that is available at any hour, which some adults report eases loneliness.

What it costs

  • Emotional reliance among young and vulnerable users.8
  • A flood of convincing fakes and falling trust in genuine material.9
  • Schoolwork that looks better without learning behind it.3

What is not known

  • Long-term effects on how children learn and relate to others. No long studies exist yet.
  • Whether heavy AI use weakens skills over years.
  • How much AI-made misinformation changes what people believe or how they vote.
