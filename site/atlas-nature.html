Nature and food

AI can listen to forests, count animals and advise farmers by phone. The promise is large. Field evidence that it works is thin.

Nature

A panel of conservation scientists and AI specialists picked 21 uses most likely to help: recognising species from images and sound, tracking the illegal wildlife trade, and easing conflict between people and wildlife.1 The same panel names risks, among them tools designed far from the places they are used and the loss of field skills.1

A 2026 paper in the Journal of Applied Ecology warns that uptake is outpacing oversight, and lists validation, data sovereignty and the environmental cost of the computing itself among the gaps.2 South African scientists ranked AI among their top ten emerging issues, as both an opportunity and a risk.3

Food

The World Bank reports early results of up to 38% higher yields and $580 more profit per hectare for Ethiopian smallholders using tailored AI advice. The figures come from a 2023 study, before today’s chat-based tools.4 One advisory tool serves more than 350,000 plots in seven African countries through a messaging app.5

The first randomised trial of AI advice for smallholders, with 1,935 rice farmers, found that farmers valued the service but saw no short-run change in yields or costs.6 A review of 51 studies found most evidence came from simulations and models, with low-income countries under-represented.7 Poor connectivity, cost and low digital literacy may leave smallholders behind while large farms gain.8

The ledger

What is gained

  • Monitoring of species and habitats at a scale people cannot manage by hand.1
  • Farm advice that reaches smallholders by phone.5

What it costs

  • Data centres’ own demand for power, water and land, covered on other pages.
  • Tools and data controlled far from the communities concerned.1
  • A widening gap between large and small farms.8

What is not known

  • Whether early yield gains hold up in rigorous trials.6
  • The net effect on biodiversity once AI’s own footprint is counted.
  • Effects of data-centre construction on wildlife. No study was found.

F3 and this topic

Not in the model

F3 does not model this. World3 models food and farmland in aggregate, with no effect of AI on yields and no biodiversity. Land and water use by data centres are planned as reported outputs (decision D-066, step E4), not built.

The figures on this page are context for readers. They do not enter any F3 run.
