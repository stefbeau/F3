Work and the economy

Many jobs will change, and few will disappear outright. The gains measured so far are real but modest, and they go mostly to rich countries and large firms.

What is happening

Jobs. The International Labour Organization finds that one worker in four is in an occupation with some exposure to generative AI, with clerical work the most exposed.1 IMF staff put the share of jobs that could be affected in some way at about 40%, which means changed tasks and skills and not necessarily lost jobs.4 Entry-level roles are more exposed than senior ones.5

The ILO itself cautions that exposure scores are not predictions of job losses.2

Growth. The OECD's main estimate is 0.25 to 0.6 percentage points of extra productivity growth a year over a decade, and up to 1.3 points for labour productivity in the most exposed G7 economies.78 AI investment added an estimated half a point to US growth in 2025.10 Yet most firms in surveys report no measurable effect on employment or productivity so far.11

Who gains. One estimate puts AI's measured gains at about 4.2% of GDP in high-income countries, 0.6% in middle-income countries and 0.1% in low-income ones.11 Many developing countries risk disruption before they see benefits.3

The ledger

What is gained

  • Higher productivity where AI is adopted, and higher pay for workers with AI skills.10
  • Past general-purpose technologies raised incomes and employment in the long run.4

What it costs

  • Women’s jobs and clerical jobs are more exposed.1
  • Fewer entry-level openings for people starting careers.5
  • Gains concentrate in rich countries and large firms.11

What is not known

  • How many exposed jobs are transformed and how many are lost.
  • When, or whether, firm-level gains show up in national statistics.
  • How fast economies can retrain and reallocate workers.

Notice (AI sector): S3's default production block (variant P, D-062, D-065) is within 2% of Epoch AI's GATE reference runs for the quantities of test A1.3 as amended by D-064, when GATE's own investment path is supplied (reference: D-063; audit/s3-playground-composite.md). It does not reproduce GATE: GATE's investment optimisation is not implemented. Under F3's own investment rule (R3) S3's absolute output (gwp, capital, compute, data-centre electricity demand) is UNVALIDATED and leaves plausible ranges within a few years (audit/s3-default-composite.md). Results computed before 2026-11-25, including the published Explorer, used variant A, which failed A1.3 (D-029).

Why the two halves of this page disagree. The reported figures count today’s jobs that current tools could affect. The F3 sector is a growth model in which investment in computing automates tasks without friction: no hiring rules, no retraining time, no limit on electricity. In the central setting it reaches nearly all tasks by 2049. The reported evidence shows most firms measuring no effect yet. F3 exists to test which limits lie between those two pictures. The sector’s economic output is not shown on this site.
