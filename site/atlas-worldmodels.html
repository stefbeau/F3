World models

Chatbots learn from text. World models learn how the physical world behaves, mostly from video, so that a machine can predict what its actions will do. They are the bet behind the next generation of robots, and still mostly research.

What it is

The idea. A world model lets a machine imagine what happens next if it acts in a certain way, then pick the action whose imagined result is closest to its goal. Yann LeCun set out this design in 2022: a system that perceives, predicts, scores the predicted futures and plans.5

Two routes. Some models generate the future frame by frame, as video or as a 3D scene. The other route, called JEPA, predicts a compressed description of the scene and skips detail that cannot be predicted.3

Money. AMI Labs, founded by LeCun after he left Meta, raised $1.03 billion in March 2026, the largest seed round in Europe to date. It plans to work first with manufacturing, biomedicine and robotics companies, and has no product yet.12 Its chief executive has himself predicted that the label will soon be used by many firms to raise money.1

What has been measured

The published results are laboratory benchmarks, reported by the teams that built the models. One model, trained without labels on more than a million hours of video and then on 62 hours of robot footage, grasped a cup in 65% of attempts against 15% for a baseline, and planned each action in about 16 seconds against about four minutes for a video-generating model.3 A 2026 paper stacks several such models to plan over longer horizons and raises success on a simulated maze task from 18% to 73%; it has not been run on a physical robot.4

The authors list limits themselves: errors build up over long predictions, results depend on camera placement, and goals must be given as images.3 A theoretical claim that such models converge on one shared representation of the world is contested by a published rebuttal.67

The ledger

What is gained

  • Robots that need far less task-specific training data, if the laboratory results carry over.3
  • Planning that can be inspected: the system scores imagined futures against a stated goal.5
  • Much of the research is published with code.

What it costs

  • Training on video at this scale needs large computing capacity, with the power and water that go with it.
  • Capital committed years before any product exists.2
  • The same capability serves military and surveillance uses.

What is not known

  • Whether laboratory results hold in homes, factories and streets.
  • Which route wins, generated video or compressed prediction, or neither.
  • The energy cost of training and running these models. No figure was found.
  • When, or whether, products arrive.

F3 and this topic

Not in the model

F3 does not model this. F3 is a world model of another kind: equations written by people about the economy and the planet, not patterns learned from video. Notes exist on borrowing ideas from learned world models for F3’s interface (a map of model states, search towards a goal). Nothing of that is built.

The figures on this page are context for readers. They do not enter any F3 run.
