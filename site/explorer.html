More World3 charts

Status: the standard run with climate damage off matches WorldDynamics.jl (tests A3.1 and A3.2 met); test A3.4(b) failed; runs with changed parameters, and all runs with climate damage on, are not compared with anything (WorldDynamics.jl has no climate damage). Population, industrial output, resources and pollution, 1900-2100; precomputed grid, 3 levers x 3 values x 4 damage states. No health or yield effect of warming is modelled.
What this panel is, and its test status
    • The levers are World3 parameters chosen as a Provisional (delegated) decision, not an approved one (D-046, docs/site-explorer.md). They are not forecasts of anything; they show how the 1900-2100 World3 run changes when one parameter changes.
    • Climate damage (D-051, D-052). When switched on, World3's industrial output is multiplied by (1 minus the damage fraction) from 2025. The damage fraction is c x T^2 with T the FaIR ensemble-mean warming above 1850-1900 from the recorded run (no new climate run per scenario) and c from the chosen D-007 option. It is a level-of-output effect, not the upper end of the literature: growth-effect estimates are larger and are not represented. No new coefficient was introduced. The change flows through every link industrial output has in World3 (investment, agriculture, resources, population), so it is not a health or yield effect of warming. With damage on, the run is not compared with WorldDynamics.jl; the M6 tests check that switching damage off returns the stand-alone World3 to 1e-9, that zero warming changes nothing, that more warming lowers industrial output, and that nothing is NaN or negative over the whole grid.
    • World3 is run as the D-031 group: its five sectors take 0.0625-year sub-steps inside the model's 0.25-year loop.
    • The 2004 parameter set is the provisional default (D-015); the 1974 set exists in the code and is not shown.
    • Resources are shown as the fraction of the starting stock that remains (the starting stock is the chosen lever value); pollution as the persistent-pollution index (1 = the 1970 level); industrial output in model dollars per year (World3's own constant dollars, not a market or price-indexed figure).
    • World3 is a single run per setting: there is no band and no uncertainty analysis.
    • No health or yield effect of warming. World3's population and food sectors have no warming term, and the four warming effects of Earth4All that would act on them (life expectancy, land yield, CO2 fertilisation and the CO2 lifetime) are deliberately omitted (D-049).
    • S3's economy and World3's are not reconciled: they are two models of the same world, and the AI-race panel does not change anything in this panel.

    All caveats for the AI-race panel, including those for the withheld charts
    • AI sector (S3). It does not reproduce GATE. GATE was re-implemented from the paper, not from its code. Epoch states that GATE behaves poorly near full automation.
    • Damage options. All three are level-of-output effects. The "high" option is NOT the upper end of the literature: growth-effect estimates are larger and are not represented (D-007).
    • Investment rule R3 is a scenario-lever rule, not a GATE replica (D-028). Against the three shipped GATE runs, with default levers and Epoch's stored composite supplied, effective compute is 43% to 72% off at worst, output 13% to 20% off, hardware up to 35% off, and the share errors reach 24 percentage points (capital) and 55 (training share) (A1.4, report only, audit/s3-r3-a1.4.md).
    • Data-centre electricity. 485 TWh (2025) is a calibration point and 415 TWh (2024) a reference value; neither validates the module, and no independent test of modelled data-centre electricity against estimates exists before 2025. The IEA 2030 figure (950 TWh) is the IEA's projection under its own assumptions, shown for comparison only. Boundary: a data centre is a facility housing servers, storage, networking equipment and associated components (IEA, Energy and AI); whether crypto-mining load is excluded from the IEA totals is [unverified].
    • Assumptions to state (D-033, D-034): the 5% data-centre supply cap and the tie of energy efficiency to S3's hardware curve are assumptions; the central and high damage coefficients are derived from published anchors, not read as printed coefficients.

    About the withheld compute and electricity charts (text kept from the earlier Demo 0 page)

    • Raw compute stock (FLOP/year, log scale) and automated fraction of tasks, for the three AI settings at the chosen damage option. Absolute compute is unvalidated against GATE after about 2027; shapes are more reliable than levels.
    • Data-centre electricity, TWh per year, chosen AI setting, central damage. "Demand" is what the installed compute would draw; "used" is capped by the share of electricity supply available to data centres (5% of the recorded Earth4All.jl TLTL electricity production, a provisional assumption). The IEA points were 2024 (415, reference) and 2025 (485, calibration point); the 2030 point (950) is the IEA's projection, not a target. Values after about 2027 inherit S3's unvalidated output; the demand curve leaves any plausible range quickly, which is a property of S3's compute path, not a finding about the world.
    • Supply binds from about 2027-2028 in this slice. The half-supply curve is not below the base curve in every year (see the recorded B1 failure below); the sign holds for the unlimited case.
    • The series are kept in data/explorer.json and in the generator; they are not drawn.

    What is connected

    Only two links are built and tested: warming lowers World3 industrial output (off by default) and warming lowers S3's own output. One more is built with its outputs withheld; four are not built. Solid, dotted and dashed lines say which. Open the connections map to inspect each of the seven links.

    What is not shown

    • Inequality, social stability, minerals, water and the full economy (these come in later milestones). The AI-race slice's economy is GATE's own, with population exogenous.
    • Uncertainty bands beyond the FaIR ensemble band; any calibration beyond the stated checks (the 2025 data-centre calibration point and the warming check, which failed); any claim about likelihood.
    • The growth-effect damage case. Climate emissions are prescribed; neither World3 nor AI electricity feeds back into emissions.
    • Any coupling other than climate damage to World3 industrial output and to S3 output (the dashed arrows in "What is connected").
    • S3 compute and data-centre electricity outputs (withheld, see the AI-race panel).
    • Open caveats: GATE is re-implemented from its paper and not from its code; Epoch states GATE behaves poorly near full automation; FaIR is used as published; nothing has been validated against an independent reference beyond the tests A1, A2 and A3 and their recorded failures.
