Limitations

Every caveat in one place, with the failed tests stated plainly. Nothing on this page has been softened; sizes are the recorded ones.

Public draft, published 2026-10-21. Conditional scenarios, not forecasts.Every curve in the Explorer is the output of a model run under stated assumptions. None is a prediction, none has a likelihood, and recorded test failures are listed on the Limitations page (plan test A2.7).

Tests that failed (recorded, not relaxed)

A1.3

What it checked: The AI sector (S3) against the three runs shipped by Epoch AI's GATE playground, within 2% at every year, with GATE's own investment path supplied

Result: Failed for all three runs. Output first exceeds 2% in 2026-27; capital, hardware, software and compute from 2027-33. With Epoch's own task-composite supplied, every quantity matches, so the gap is S3's task-allocation step; its cause is unresolved. Three different discretisations of that step gave the same failure. S3's absolute output (output, capital, effective compute) is unvalidated after about 2027. S3 does not reproduce GATE.

A1.4

What it checked: The investment rule R3 against GATE's own investment path (report only, no pass or fail)

Result: Poor agreement. With Epoch's stored composite supplied, effective compute is 43% to 72% off at worst and output 13% to 20% off; hardware up to 35% off; investment shares up to 24 percentage points off (capital) and 55 (training share). R3 is a scenario-lever rule, not a GATE replica; it was fitted to the same three runs.

A2.1

What it checked: FaIR's ensemble-mean warming for 2015-2024 against HadCRUT5's mean 95% interval

Result: Failed. FaIR gives 1.196 K above 1850-1900; the HadCRUT5 interval is 1.234 to 1.305 K. FaIR is 0.037 K below the lower edge and 0.073 K below the best estimate. 2023 and 2024 (outside the ensemble's calibration window) run 0.16 to 0.20 K low; the cause is untested. Scenario warming therefore starts about 0.04 to 0.07 K below observed for the current decade.

A2.6

What it checked: Halving the loop step (0.25 to 0.125 year) changes the slice's outputs by at most 0.1%

Result: Failed. Largest difference 0.565% in data-centre electricity (first above 0.1% in 2028); output 0.197%; compute 0.108%.

B1 sign

What it checked: Lowering electricity supply lowers compute growth afterwards

Result: Failed as written. Halving supply gave up to 17.8% more compute in 2038-2045. The suspected cause (the investment rule depends on usable compute) is inferred, not tested.

R1 sign

What it checked: Raising the AI investment share raises compute and automation

Result: Failed on automation: both runs are at 1.0 by 2065, so no increase can be seen (compute is higher).

A3.4(b)

What it checked: World3's population sector inside the full model equals the same sector run alone with recorded inputs, within 0.25%

Result: Failed. Up to 1.019% (1974 parameter set) and 0.931% (2004 set). The reading that it reflects small differences in the other four sectors, not an error in the population equations, is inferred, not tested.

A4.5

What it checked: (Not shown in the Explorer.) The ported Earth4All economy sectors together reproduce the Earth4All.jl economy within 2%

Result: Failed in both scenarios from about 2076 (GDP up to 2.5%, inequality up to 3.2%); accepted as recorded by the editor.

What is, and is not, compared with anything

  • World3: only the standard parameters, with climate damage off, were compared with WorldDynamics.jl. The Explorer's runs with changed parameters have not been compared with any reference. Extreme settings (for example a land yield factor of 2.0, which ends with a population of about 0.4 billion) show what the ported equations do under that input; nothing says it is right.
  • AI race: unvalidated against GATE after about 2027 (above). Compute and data-centre electricity outputs are withheld because they leave any plausible range (in the recorded runs modelled data-centre demand reaches about 7 billion TWh a year by 2100, against about 485 TWh in 2025; the earlier recorded figures were about 20,000 TWh in 2030 and 414,000 TWh in 2040); that is a property of S3's compute path, not a finding about the world.
  • Data-centre module: 485 TWh (2025) is a calibration point, 415 TWh (2024) a reference value; neither validates the module, and no independent test of modelled data-centre electricity against estimates exists before 2025. The IEA's 950 TWh for 2030 is the IEA's projection under its own assumptions. The IEA's formal definition of a data centre could not be verified: whether crypto-mining load is excluded from its totals is [unverified]. The 5% cap on the share of electricity supply available to data centres and the tie of energy efficiency to S3's hardware curve are assumptions.
  • Climate: FaIR is used as published. Non-CO2 emissions and land-use CO2 are prescribed, not modelled. The ensemble was constrained to data through 2022. The band is the ensemble band only.
  • Damages: the three options are level-of-output effects (Nordhaus 2017, Barrage and Nordhaus 2024, Howard and Sterner 2017). "High" is not the upper end of the literature: growth-effect estimates (for example Burke, Hsiang and Miguel 2015, whose abstract reports about 23% lower average global incomes by 2100) are larger and are not represented; their coefficients were not readable in the copy obtained and are [unverified]. The central and high coefficients are derived from printed anchors, not read as printed coefficients. The temperature baseline of Nordhaus (2017) is not stated in the text read [unverified]; FaIR's baseline is 1850-1900 and no offset is applied. Shares are of S3's own output, whose level is unvalidated.
  • Climate damage to World3 industrial output (D-051, D-052). A level-of-output effect, off by default, from the D-007 damage function with the recorded FaIR ensemble-mean warming; it multiplies World3's whole industrial output, which feeds investment, agriculture, resources and population. Damage-on runs are not compared with WorldDynamics.jl, which has no climate damage. High is not the upper end of the literature: growth-effect estimates are larger and are not represented. No new coefficient was introduced. S3's economy and World3's are not reconciled: the AI-race run and World3 are not connected to each other. What was tested: switching damage off returns the stand-alone World3 to 1e-9; zero warming equals the baseline; more warming lowers industrial output; no value is NaN or negative on the whole grid.
  • No health or yield effect of warming: the warming effects on life expectancy and on land yield (and the CO2 effect on yield and on the CO2 lifetime) that Earth4All has are omitted by decision (D-049); World3's population and food have no warming term, so warming does not reach them. To be revisited.
  • Not shown at all: population (beyond World3's own), any other social measure, social stability, minerals, water, the full economy, uncertainty bands beyond the FaIR ensemble band, any likelihood.

Model versus reality: a comparison, not a validation

  • Not a validation. The Model versus reality charts set World3 and FaIR against observed series. They were not fitted to them, nothing was tuned to improve the match, and the bands used in the sentences (5% for population, 25% for the output index) are plain-language aids written before the numbers were seen, not acceptance criteria of the plan.
  • Population. World3 is the 2004 parameter set. Test A3.4(b) FAILED (S1 inside the full run differs from S1 alone by up to 0.93% against 0.25%). Runs with changed parameters are not compared with WorldDynamics.jl. The UN gives estimates to 2023; later years are its projection and are not used in the gap.
  • Industrial output against GDP. Not the same quantity: World3 industrial output is industry only, in World3's own model dollars; world GDP covers all sectors in constant 2015 US dollars. They are indexed to the same year and nothing else, so the gap is not an error rate, and a close match would not mean the model is right.
  • Temperature. Test A2.1 FAILED. The FaIR ensemble was constrained to temperatures up to 2022, so 1850-2022 is in sample and the comparison there is not independent; only 2023-2025 is out of sample. Emissions from 2025 are prescribed and non-CO2 emissions are not modelled. HadCRUT5 is one observational estimate; GISTEMP was used only as a cross-check and is not shown.

Warming map: illustrative downscaling

  • Illustrative downscaling of the global FaIR result. Not a regional climate projection. Pattern-scaling multiplies the global warming by a fixed coefficient per region: it ignores regional feedbacks, aerosols and ocean dynamics, and assumes that the pattern of warming does not change with the amount or the speed of global warming. Real regional warming also depends on the emissions path (for example aerosols), which is prescribed here.
  • Uncertainty. Only the global FaIR uncertainty is shown. The spread of the coefficients across 32 CMIP6 models (pattern uncertainty), internal variability and the difference between the models' temperature and FaIR's are not shown.
  • Coefficients are derived by F3 from the regional aggregates of the IPCC AR6 Atlas; they are not an IPCC product. Resolution is the 58 AR6 reference regions, not a grid. The area-weighted consistency check is met with a small margin (see Methods) and no coefficient was rescaled.
  • Not produced: country-level results, damage or population maps. World3, FaIR and S3 are global models; a map of their output at country level would be invented.

Provenance of choices

  • GATE was re-implemented from the paper (arXiv:2503.04941 v2), not from Epoch's code. Epoch states that GATE behaves poorly near full automation.
  • Some design choices are Provisional (delegated), not approved by the editor (for example the World3 levers, D-046, and the data-centre module, D-032 to D-036). They are listed in the project's decision register.
  • The Explorer is a public draft, published 2026-10-21; it carries Provisional (delegated) choices that the editor may still change.
