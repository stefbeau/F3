Methods

What is run, how it is stepped, and how to read what you see. For what is wrong or unvalidated, read Limitations.

Public draft, published 2026-10-21. Conditional scenarios, not forecasts.Every curve in the Explorer is the output of a model run under stated assumptions. None is a prediction, none has a likelihood, and recorded test failures are listed on the Limitations page (plan test A2.7).

The three model pieces

  • World3 (The Limits to Growth, 1974 version and its 2004 update). F3 contains a Python port of five World3 sectors (population, agriculture, capital, non-renewable resources, persistent pollution), ported from WorldDynamics.jl v1.0.0 (MIT). The Explorer shows the 2004 parameter set, the provisional default (D-015). The reference environment is a pinned registry snapshot of 2024-04-25 with default solver options (D-017). The standard run was compared with WorldDynamics.jl: every state of the four ported sectors within 0.124% (A3.1) and all 29 states within 2% (A3.2, worst 1.906%).
  • Climate: FaIR 2.2.4 (Apache 2.0), unmodified, with the published fair-calibrate v1.4.1 constrained ensemble of 841 members (Smith et al., 2024). Emissions are prescribed: CO2 from the Global Carbon Budget 2025 for 2023-2024, and all other gases and later years from a scenario file of the ensemble's own; no F3 sector produces them (D-021).
  • AI race: S3, a re-implementation of the laws of motion, automation and production of GATE (Erdil et al., arXiv:2503.04941) written from the paper, not from Epoch's code, driven by an investment rule R3 (D-028), plus a data-centre electricity module and a damage function (D-007). Withheld outputs are described on the page.

Time and coupling

  • The model loop steps by 0.25 year (D-003). Sectors exchange values at step boundaries; each sector reads the other sectors' values at the start of the step and holds them across it (explicit coupling, D-022). t = Y is 1 January of year Y; stocks are values on that date.
  • World3 sub-steps (D-031). The five World3 sectors are advanced together as one group in 0.0625-year sub-steps inside each 0.25-year loop step, with the same start-of-sub-step coupling among them. With plain 0.25-year coupling the full-run test A3.2 failed (worst state 6.90% for the 1974 set and 7.96% for the 2004 set, against 2%); at 0.0625 year no state exceeded 2%. The Sector interface and D-003 and D-022 are unchanged.
  • The AI sector advances in whole-year periods (GATE's own convention); the climate sector is re-run from 1750 every year with all 841 members and nothing carried between years (D-021 option a, chosen by the editor after measuring the alternatives).
  • One coupling between the pieces (D-051, D-052): climate damage to World3 industrial output, off by default. The climate path is a single recorded run replayed (exact, because emissions are prescribed and no lever changes them). When climate damage is switched on in the World3 panel, World3's industrial output is multiplied by (1 minus c x T^2) from 2025, with T the recorded FaIR ensemble-mean warming above 1850-1900 and c one of the three D-007 coefficients; the multiplier is held across the 0.0625-year sub-steps of each loop step (explicit coupling, D-022). The AI-race run takes the same recorded warming for its own damage option. World3 and the AI-race run are not connected to each other: S3's economy and World3's are two models of the same world and are not reconciled. Tests of the coupling: switching it off returns the stand-alone World3 to 1e-9; zero warming equals the baseline; more warming lowers industrial output (named output and window, with a control that fails); nothing is NaN or negative over the whole grid.

What is connected: the roadmap

Three pieces and the links between them. It complements the interactive connections map, which gives the seven links one by one with their effect numbers and tests.

  • Solid: built and tested
  • Dotted: built, outputs withheld
  • Dashed: not built
  • Solid: built and tested. The first coupling is climate damage to World3 industrial output (default off); the warming-to-S3 link is the AI-race run's damage option.
  • Dashed: not built. Nothing here claims that the AI model changes World3 or that either changes the climate.

Scenario grids

  • Units of World3 industrial output. The industrial-output chart is in model dollars per year: World3's own constant dollars, as the model defines them. They are not market prices, not tied to a year's price level, and not comparable with S3's output, which is a separate economy in separate units.
  • The Explorer is a static page of precomputed runs (D-009): no model runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.
  • World3: 3 levers x 3 values = 27 runs (D-046, Provisional) x 4 climate-damage states (off, low, central, high) = 108 runs: initial non-renewable resources (0.5e12, 1e12 standard, 2e12), land yield factor after 1975 (1.0 standard, 1.5, 2.0), persistent-pollution generation factor after 1975 (1.0 standard, 0.5, 0.25).
  • AI race: 3 investment settings (low, central, race; levers of the R3 rule) x 3 damage options = 9 runs, plus two electricity-supply cases kept in the data for the sign test.

How to read a band

  • The shaded band on the climate chart is the 5th to 95th percentile across the 841 members of the published constrained ensemble; the line is their mean. Each member has its own climate parameters and its own seeded internal variability. It describes the spread of that ensemble under the prescribed emissions. It is not a probability statement about the real climate and not an uncertainty analysis of the whole model.
  • World3 and S3 are single runs with no band: each curve is one run under the stated settings. A different parameter, or a different version of the model, gives a different curve, and the Explorer does not say how likely any curve is.
  • Where a curve is dashed grey (World3 panel) it is the standard run with damage off, shown for comparison when you change a lever or switch damage on; a dotted curve is the same lever settings with damage off.
  • Takeaway cards are sentences and numbers computed by the page from the same data as the charts (peaks, end values, first years), so they cannot disagree with the charts. They describe a run; they are not findings about the world.

Reading and downloading a chart

  • Hover, touch or press the left and right arrow keys on a focused chart to read the values for a year (shift with the arrows steps ten years). Each chart can be downloaded as a PNG or as a CSV of its curves; the downloads carry the caveats, including the climate-damage caveat when damage is on.
  • The chosen scenario is kept in the address (the part after #), so a link restores it. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.
  • The data. Everything the charts draw is in data/explorer.json, strict JSON in which null means no value (never zero). The pages load the identical text as a script, data/explorer.js, because a browser blocks a page opened from disk from reading a local JSON file; the generator writes both from the same string and a test checks they match. The file also holds the series that are withheld from the charts (S3 compute, data-centre electricity).

Observed history overlay (Model versus reality)

  • What it is. Three charts set model output against observed history: World3 population against the UN's world population (1950-2025); World3 industrial output against world GDP, both indexed to 1990 = 1; the FaIR hindcast against HadCRUT5 (1850-2025). It is a comparison, not a validation. Nothing in any model was tuned to the observed series, and no parameter, equation or run was changed to produce these charts.
  • Series. UN World Population Prospects 2024, world total, medium variant, 1 July (estimates to 2023, UN projection after); World Bank WDI NY.GDP.MKTP.KD, world, constant 2015 US dollars; HadCRUT.5.2.0.0 annual global mean, re-based to the mean of its annual best estimates for 1850-1900, so that observed and FaIR anomalies share a baseline (the FaIR anomalies use the notebook's weighted 1850-1900 mean). Versions, retrieval dates, licences, URLs and checksums: Sources and audit/data/sources-history.json.
  • Cross-check. NASA GISTEMP v4, re-based to 1880-1900 like HadCRUT5, differs from it by a mean absolute 0.08 K over 1880-2025 (largest 0.22 K, in 1937). Its licence terms for the data table were not found, so no GISTEMP value is shown.
  • FaIR hindcast. The same published 841-member configuration as the climate chart under Details on the Explorer, started in 1750 and run to 2026 so that calendar year 2025 exists; calendar-year value = mean of the time bounds at the start and end of the year (the method of test A2.1). From 2025 the prescribed medium-extension emissions apply.
  • Conventions. World3 values sit at 1 January; a calendar-year value is the mean of the values at the start and end of the year. The history charts use the damage-off run of the chosen World3 settings.
  • Sentences. The sentence under each chart is computed from the data shown. The year compared, the 5% (population) and 25% (output index) bands and the temperature test (FaIR mean inside HadCRUT5's 95% interval, the form of A2.1) were fixed before the numbers were seen (docs/history-comparison.md); the bands are plain-language aids set by the project, Provisional (D-057), not acceptance criteria of the plan.

Warming map (pattern scaling, illustrative)

Illustrative downscaling of the global FaIR result. Not a regional climate projection. Pattern-scaling ignores regional feedbacks, aerosols, ocean dynamics.
  • Method. Regional warming in a year = the global FaIR warming of that year (above 1850-1900; ensemble mean, 5th and 95th percentile of the recorded 841-member run) x a fixed coefficient for the region, in kelvin of local warming per kelvin of global warming. The same recorded climate run feeds the map in every scenario: climate damage does not change warming.
  • Coefficients. For each of the 58 IPCC AR6 reference regions (46 land, 12 ocean), the multi-model mean of the least-squares slope of the region's annual mean near-surface air temperature on the global annual mean, for 32 CMIP6 models (historical followed by SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 pooled; one run per model). Land regions use land values, ocean regions sea values, the three land-ocean regions both. Input: the regionally aggregated CMIP6 monthly data of the IPCC AR6 WGI Atlas repository (CC BY 4.0). No redistributable gridded pattern file was found, so the AR6 regions are the resolution of the map. The Atlas does not publish the slopes: they are computed by F3 (site_src/map_build.py, fixed in docs/warming-map.md before it ran). The intercept is ignored, so the regional value is also taken to be "above 1850-1900".
  • Consistency check. The area-weighted mean of the coefficients over all regions (weights: spherical area of the region polygons) must be 1 within 2%. Result: 1.0177 (deviation +1.77%), met. It is close to the limit: land-only and sea-only values are applied to polygons that contain both, and the same check with the land-and-sea file for every region gives 1.0005. No coefficient was rescaled. Per model the same quantity lies between 1.005 and 1.031. Sign checks: Arctic Ocean coefficient 3.00 (above 1), land mean 1.27 against ocean mean 0.81.
  • What is shown, what is not. The 5th to 95th percentile beside the map is the global FaIR uncertainty. Pattern uncertainty (the spread of each coefficient across models) is stored in site/data/warming-map.json and is not drawn. Surface air temperature in the CMIP6 data is treated as the same quantity as FaIR's surface temperature; this is an assumption. No country-level or population or damage map is produced.
  • Display. Colour scale ColorBrewer OrRd (sequential, colour-blind safe), the same scale in every year, running from 0 K to the largest regional value of the ensemble mean in 2100. Region outlines are simplified; coastlines are Natural Earth 1:110m (public domain), simplified.
