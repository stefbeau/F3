Average or spread?

Public draft, published 2026-10-21. Conditional scenarios, not forecasts.Every curve in the Explorer is the output of a model run under stated assumptions. None is a prediction, none has a likelihood, and recorded test failures are listed on the Limitations page (plan test A2.7).
A hypothesis, not a result of the F3 model. Nothing on this page is computed by F3 and nothing here is built into it. The claim that AI widens gaps on long, delegated tasks is untested; most published evidence so far shows gaps narrowing. The figures about other systems come from the published studies listed at the foot of the page. They were read from search extracts on 2026-11-12; the primary pages were not opened.

Why this question is on the F3 site

An average can hide what matters. F3 already reports bands instead of single lines. This page asks a related question about AI: does it pull people and firms toward the average, or push them apart?

The page states one hypothesis, shows what would count against it, and checks whether the same pattern appears in six other systems. It is background for a possible extension of F3, described at the end. It is not part of the model.

The hypothesis: the effect of AI on gaps depends on task length

Schematic, not drawn from data. Solid: found in published field studies. Dashed: predicted, not tested.

On short tasks with a person in every step, AI assistants have lifted weaker performers most, so the gap between strong and weak narrowed. The hypothesis is that on long tasks delegated to AI agents the effect reverses: differences in judgment are multiplied and the gap widens.

  • Gap narrowed: customer support. Productivity up 14% on average and 34% for novice and low-skilled workers. Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, Generative AI at Work.
  • Gap narrowed: consulting. Below-average performers improved by 43%, above-average by 17%. Dell'Acqua and colleagues, Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier.
  • Gap widened: small businesses in Kenya. No average effect; low performers did nearly 10% worse, high performers may have gained over 15%. Otis and colleagues, Management Science.

What would count against it: controlled studies of multi-day delegated tasks that also show the gap narrowing.

Four kinds of spread, kept apart

"More variance" can mean four different things. The hypothesis is about the third: the spread of outcomes between people and firms. Evidence for one kind is not evidence for another.

The same question in six other systems: it fits two

Solid outline: the spread itself has widened, or the picture is mixed. Dashed outline: the change is a shifted average or a rising count, not a wider spread.

  • Rainfall (fits). Variability has grown over about 75% of land since 1900; daily variability up 1.2% per decade. Zhang, Zhou and Wu, Science, 2024 (AAAS summary).
  • Firm productivity (fits). In manufacturing, frontier firms gained 2.8% a year from 2001 to 2013; all other firms gained 0.6%. Andrews, Criscuolo and Gal, OECD (authors' summary).
  • Incomes (mixed). Gaps between countries have narrowed; gaps within countries have stayed roughly constant on average since 2000. Brookings, The present and future of global inequality.
  • Political polarization (mixed). Among twelve OECD countries since the 1980s, hostility between party supporters rose most in the United States, rose less in five others and fell in six. Boxell, Gentzkow and Shapiro, NBER working paper 26669.
  • Heat extremes (does not fit). Hot extremes have become more frequent and more intense since the 1950s; cold extremes less frequent and less severe. IPCC AR6 findings, as summarised by the Hong Kong Observatory.
  • Armed conflict (does not fit). 65 conflicts involving a state in 2025, the most since records began in 1946; 31 in 2010. UCDP, Journal of Peace Research.

A shifted average and a wider spread look alike from the tail. Heat extremes are more common while cold extremes retreat, which points to a moved average; rainfall is different, its spread itself has widened. Each system has its own cause, and none of these changes was caused by AI. The reading of heat extremes as mostly a shifted average is this page's inference, not a statement quoted from the source.

What this could mean for F3 (proposed, not built)

  • Show spread wherever the model produces it. The climate panel already draws the 841-member band. Other panels show single runs.
  • A switchable assumption, not a fact. If an AI link to inequality between firms or workers is ever added, it would need both settings: gaps narrow, gaps widen.
  • Tag indicators. For each observed series, say whether its spread is widening or its average is shifting.

None of this is built or decided. It is recorded as a proposal in decision D-062 for the editor.
